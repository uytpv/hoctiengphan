 Instructions for your Finnish language studies 
 Welcome to start (or continue) your Finnish studies! 
 The Finnish language opens up a new way of perceiving the world: Finnish is a language with 
 a very diﬀerent structure and vocabulary than, for example, Indo-European languages. 
 Therefore,  progress is slower at ﬁrst. However, perseverance  is rewarded!  Finnish is 
 diﬀerent but no harder to learn than other foreign languages. 
 Here are some instructions for your Finnish studies. Please read them carefully. 
 They are intended to help you to create a good learning attitude which is the key 
 to learning. 
 Make studying Finnish a habit.  Spend at least 15-30  minutes a day studying 
 Finnish. You will be surprised at how fast you start to see results of your daily 
 routine. It does not have to be just studying by the book but you can do fun 
 things with the language as well (tips below). Try to ﬁnd your inner motivation. 
 When you are motivated to learn, you will learn.  Finnish  is not a difﬁcult 
 language but only a different one. Motivated students have always learned and 
 will always learn, no matter what the language. 
 Build a strong basis.  As in any language, you have  to build a solid basis for your 
 future studies. This means regular studying and continuous revision of the 
 previously studied topics. Unlike some other courses, Finnish courses are not 
 separate courses but are always built on prior knowledge. For instance, on 
 Finnish 2 when learning how to express dates in Finnish, the teacher will expect 
 you to remember the months which you learned on Finnish 1. 
 Attend the lessons.  If you miss one or two lessons,  it will still be worth coming 
 back (teacher will not be angry). If you just stay at home without studying, you 
 will not learn anything, whereas if you attend the lessons, you will probably learn 
 many things. The experience has shown that it is the actively participating 
 students who get the best test results and grades. Attending the lessons really 
 does help. 
 Participate actively during the lessons  . Listen to the teacher, make notes and 
 ask questions. Participate in class activities. Forget about your phone, social 
 media and assignments from other courses. You cannot concentrate on learning 

=== PAGE 2 ===
 Finnish and doing something else at the same time. Since you are there in the 
 class, it is only wise to concentrate on Finnish instead of something else. 
 Attend the supervised assignment (= test) occasions.  Skipping the supervised 
 assignment is guaranteed to give you 0 points, whereas taking the test will 
 probably give you at least some points. In addition, if you miss the supervised 
 assignments you will have to take the re-exam covering the whole course, i.e. 
 four or ﬁve chapters of the book. It is much easier to do the tests in two parts in 
 the two supervised assignments.  You cannot take the re-exam without having 
 attended at least one of the two supervised assignments.  You cannot retake a 
 separate supervised assignment. 
 Do the homework.  As a university level student, you  are expected to do quite 
 much studying on your own as well. There will be homework after each lesson. 
 The book’s exercises as well as many Moodle and online exercises (H5P, Quizlet, 
 Wordwall) will be provided. It may sometimes seem like a lot of exercises but 
 that is the point: you are supposed to have enough material for a regular, daily 
 practice. So, remember to share the workload and do not try to do all the 
 homework the evening before the course. 
 Get the continuous assessment points.  Continuous assessment makes up 40 % 
 of the course total. If you, for some reason, do not do well in the written tests, 
 you will still have the possibility to pass the course thanks to the continuous 
 assessment. Continuous assessment consists of  demonstrating  your oral skills 
 (listening, speaking) during the lessons and  doing the weekly assignments  and 
 homework  . This is a way to demonstrate your skills in another way than in a 
 written test. 
 In conclusion, the teacher will provide you with all the necessary materials, 
 instructions and explanations. However,  the teacher  cannot do the learning for 
 you  .  Learning is your job.  As a university level student,  studying is your main 
 task and your studies is what you should prioritize. 
 Some remarks 
 Concerning spoken and written language.  Why do we  not study the spoken 
 language? Spoken language (  puhekieli  ) is important.  It is also something you are 
 guaranteed to learn outside the classroom as you talk to people and listen to 
 Finns talk to each other. The spoken language is different in different parts of 

=== PAGE 3 ===
 Finland, so if you visit another part of the country or even move to somewhere 
 else, you will notice that people talk in a different way. It would be impossible to 
 study all the dialects and ways of speaking Finnish during the course. That is one 
 of the reasons why on Finnish courses, the standard Finnish is studied. 
 In order to become ﬂuent in Finnish, you will have to master the standard 
 language. It is the language of the ofﬁcial contexts (work and institutions) as well 
 as that of news and written texts. Not mastering the standard Finnish rules out 
 many jobs and stops you from ever moving up from the language level B1. 
 It is completely suitable for a non-Finn to use the standard language when 
 speaking as well. It does not sound weird at all. On the contrary, using many 
 spoken language phrases and slang words may sound very comical coming from 
 a person not otherwise ﬂuent in Finnish. In conclusion, you can manage by using 
 only the standard language but you cannot manage by mastering only the 
 spoken language. 
 Concerning the grammar.  Why do we have to study grammar?  In any language, 
 it is much more efﬁcient to memorize the general rule than to study each word 
 and form separately. In Finnish, for instance, if you learn the general rule 
 concerning the verb types, you will quite quickly learn how to conjugate any 
 Finnish verb. You can of course try to learn the language in the same way as little 
 children learn their native language, i.e. word by word. However, that would 
 require much more time which we, unfortunately, do not have on the course. 
 In addition, the grammar, i.e. the general rule, is something that will be quite 
 impossible to learn outside the classroom. Try asking a random Finn to explain 
 the different partitive endings in words  puhelin  ta  and  tietokone  tta  . They would 
 not be able to do that, just as you probably would not be able to explain in detail 
 the grammar of your native language. A random Finn cannot explain to you the 
 subtleties of the grammar (because it is not their job) but you need a trained 
 professional, i.e. the Finnish teacher, to do that. 
 Concerning the writing and video assignments.  When  doing writing or 
 speaking assignments, write/speak  in your own words  .  Do not use translation 
 programs or have your Finnish friend write the text for you. The teacher wants 
 to see how well  you  have learned the things you were  supposed to learn so far.

 In addition, it is crucial to bear in mind that e.g. Google translator is not a 
 reliable tool when translating to Finnish. The translator may be a good tool 
 between big languages such as English and French but does not work well when 
 translating to Finnish. Using translation tools in Finnish usually results in strange 
 and even comic results. Translating single words is simpler but there as well, you 
 should use a real dictionary and not Google translator. Some good dictionaries: 
 MOT dictionary (  https://www-sanakirja-ﬁ.ezproxy.haaga-helia.ﬁ/  ),  Redfox 
 sanakirja  and  Sanakirja.org  . 
 Concerning the timing of your studies.  The Finnish  courses are scheduled in 
 your study program in the following way: 
 1.  ﬁrst semester - Finnish language and culture 1 
 2.  second semester - Finnish language and culture 2 
 3.  third semester - Finnish language and culture 3 
 If you fail to follow this schedule, the Finnish course will not suit your schedule 
 anymore but you will, as a result, always have another important course 
 conﬂicting with the Finnish course. It will become quite difﬁcult to do the 
 Finnish course then. So, it is better to stick to the original plan. 
 Concerning Finnish studies elsewhere and after Haaga-Helia.  Even in Helsinki, 
 there are only so many Finnish course organizers. Most Finnish courses are 
 provided by schools and universities for their students. So, as a Haaga-Helia 
 student, you should try to make the most of it, also concerning your Finnish 
 studies. Later on, you will have to pay for your Finnish courses (e.g. 115 euros/5 
 cr) or be prepared for really intensive studies (e.g. 5 cr/3 weeks). 
 In conclusion, let us review the reasons to study Finnish. 
 The Finnish language is the key to life in Finland. Even the slightest language skills help to get 
 in touch with local people and feel part of the community. The more you learn the language, 
 the be er you will be able to func on in your daily life and aﬀect your well-being and future. 
 Proﬁciency in the Finnish language is valued in working life, and in a very large number of 
 workplaces, knowledge of Finnish is a prerequisite for employment. 
 ★  Everyday life in Finland will be easier if you know at least the basics of 
 Finnish. 
 ★  Integrating in society will be easier. 

=== PAGE 5 ===
 ★  You may survive in English but casual chatting is valued and done in 
 Finnish at many workplaces. 
 ★  Finns really appreciate your efforts and will have a positive attitude 
 towards you who are so actively studying the language. 
 On the course, we concentrate on things you cannot learn outside the 
 classroom. However,  there are many things you can learn outside the 
 classroom  . In fact, the more aware you are of your  Finnish environment and the 
 more you try to learn from that environment, the more you will learn. Use all the 
 possibilities to learn the language. Be creative and try to ﬁnd out which learning 
 method suits you the best. 
 Watch a video  about a (very hardworking) student of  Finnish and get good tips 
 for learning:  . How I Taught Myself a Difﬁcult Language
 Remember that regularity is important. Do something with Finnish every day. It 
 does not have to mean hours of studying (nothing wrong with it though, quite 
 the opposite) but you should also try to come up with something fun to do with 
 the language. Here are  some additional tips  : 
 ★  Listen to Finnish music.  The melody and the rhythm  will help you to 
 memorize the words and phrases. The teacher will provide a list of Finnish 
 songs in Moodle. 
 ★  Read signs, labels, instructions, warnings,  everything  you see when you 
 move around in the city, are cooking a meal at home or waiting in a lobby. 
 ★  Watch Finnish tv-programs and ﬁlms.  You will ﬁnd  many programs in Yle 
 Areena (kotimaiset sarjat, kotimaiset elokuvat). 
 ★  Use the Ylen kielikoulu app.  You will get instructions  from your teacher. 
 ★  Download a language learning app  , e.g. Duolingo, Memrise,  and use it 
 daily. 
 ★  Change the language of your phone in Finnish.  (Just  make sure you know 
 how to change it back before doing that.) 
 ★  Start speaking Finnish right away.  Do not be afraid  of using Finnish. Start 
 with simple sentences like greetings. Use them everywhere and with 
 everyone. Remember that when you speak, you cannot think too much 
 about grammar or aim at ﬂawless language. Every Finn will understand 
 you even if you do not say it correctly.
